CONCEPTS

Management basics

What this industry does and the words it uses.

4
What is MDM?

Mobile Device Management means controlling phones and tablets from a central console instead of touching each one. An admin enrolls devices once, then pushes apps, policies and updates remotely — across ten units or ten thousand. Without MDM, every change is manual labor per device; with it, one action covers the fleet.

UEM vs MDM vs EMM

Three acronyms for overlapping ideas. MDM (Mobile Device Management) covers phones and tablets. EMM (Enterprise Mobility Management) is Android-focused management with app and content controls. UEM (Unified Endpoint Management) extends the same thinking to every endpoint — laptops, desktops, IoT and phones. FieldX sits in the MDM/EMM space with UEM-style breadth across Android, Apple and Linux.

Ownership models

Who owns the device decides how strictly you may lock it. Corporate-owned units accept full control — lockdown, wipe, kiosk. BYOD (personal devices) gets partial control through work profiles that wall off company data without touching photos and messages. Shared devices (classrooms, nurse carts, POS) optimize for fast handover and tamper resistance. Pick the model first; every policy choice follows from it.

Management modes

On Android, control depth depends on provisioning. Device-owner mode (QR, Knox, ADB enrollment) grants full-device control including wipe and kiosk. Profile-owner mode manages only the work profile, leaving personal space alone — the BYOD compromise. Samsung Knox adds hardware-backed extras on supported devices. Stronger modes need more setup (often factory reset); weaker modes enroll with less friction.

How FieldX fits together

The platform in four parts, plus what makes it different.

4
Console, agents, channel, server

FieldX has four moving parts. The console is the web portal where admins enroll devices, build groups, write restrictions and watch reports. On-device agents (Manager, Updater, Kiosk, Store) enforce those decisions locally. A persistent channel carries commands instantly instead of waiting for check-ins. Behind it all, FieldX-hosted or private servers store inventory, policies and history.

What FieldX does differently

Four traits shape the product. No Google dependency — enrollment, push and updates work without Play Services or Firebase, including de-Googled builds and blocked regions. Instant commands over a persistent connection instead of polling delays. Encrypted SMS fallback keeps lock, wipe and reboot working with zero data. CDN firmware delivery with staged rollout and rollback for custom builds.

A day with FieldX

Morning: dashboard shows overnight compliance and offline units. Midday: a new store batch enrols by QR into its group and inherits policies automatically. Afternoon: a staged app update rolls to 10% while signage kiosks report uptime. Evening: scheduled reports land in inboxes. The pattern repeats: enroll once, target by group, verify by report.

Terms you will meet

Group, Zone and Bucket organize devices at different levels. Restriction, Policy and Order control behavior with priority. Kiosk, OTA, Watchdog, Geofence and App Limits are the headline features. Hover any dotted term anywhere in these docs for its one-line definition.
