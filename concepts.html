CONCEPTS

Management basics

What this industry does, the words it uses, and the loop every device travels.

5
What is MDM?

Managing phones, tablets and other endpoints from a central console instead of touching each one. Enroll a device once; afterwards apps, settings, policies and updates go out over the air — to ten units or ten thousand.

Every MDM product does three jobs: configure devices without handling them, secure company data on devices you don't fully own, and maintain fleets — updates, apps, health — at scale. Without MDM each change is manual labor per device; with it, one action covers the fleet.

UEM vs MDM vs EMM

Overlapping names for the same idea at different widths. MDM (Mobile Device Management) covers phones and tablets. EMM (Enterprise Mobility Management) is Android-centered management with app and content controls. UEM (Unified Endpoint Management) stretches the idea to every endpoint — laptops, desktops, IoT.

FieldX lives in the MDM/EMM space with UEM-style breadth: Android first, plus Apple and Linux fleets from the same console.

Ownership models

Who owns the device decides how far your control may go. Corporate-owned units accept everything — lockdown, wipe, kiosk (think retail POS or warehouse scanners). BYOD — personal phones — gets partial control through a work profile that walls off company data without touching photos and messages. Shared devices — classrooms, nurse carts — optimize for fast handover and tamper resistance.

Choose the model before writing a single policy; every later decision follows from it.

Management modes

On Android, control depth depends on how the device was provisioned. Device-owner mode (QR, Knox or ADB enrollment) grants full-device control including wipe and kiosk. Profile-owner mode manages only the work profile and leaves the personal side alone — the BYOD compromise. Samsung Knox adds hardware-backed extras on supported devices.

The tradeoff is setup cost: stronger modes usually need a factory reset, weaker modes enroll with less friction.

The device lifecycle

Every managed device travels the same loop. Enroll it once, by whichever method fits. It joins groups — manually or through auto-grouping rules — and inherits their policies. It then lives under monitoring, where dashboards, alerts and instant actions keep it healthy. Reports turn that history into proof for audits and SLAs. When its tour ends, it leaves cleanly: unenroll to release it, factory-reset to wipe it for the next user.

How FieldX fits together

The platform in four parts, what makes it different, and how policies travel.

5
Console, agents, channel, server

Four moving parts. The console is the web portal where admins enroll devices, build groups, write restrictions and read reports. On-device agents (Manager, Updater, Kiosk, Store) enforce those decisions locally. A persistent channel carries commands instantly instead of waiting for check-ins. Behind it all, FieldX-hosted or private servers store inventory, policies and history.

What FieldX does differently

No Google dependency — enrollment, push and updates work without Play Services or Firebase, including de-Googled builds and blocked regions. Instant commands over a persistent connection instead of polling delays. Encrypted SMS fallback keeps lock, wipe and reboot working with zero data. CDN firmware delivery with staged rollout and rollback for custom builds.

A day with FieldX

Morning: the dashboard shows overnight compliance and offline units. Midday: a new store batch enrolls by QR into its group and inherits policies automatically. Afternoon: a staged app update rolls to 10% while signage kiosks report uptime. Evening: scheduled reports land in inboxes. The pattern never changes: enroll once, target by group, verify by report.

How a policy reaches a device

Follow one restriction from thought to enforcement. You write it once in the console and attach it to a group — never to individual devices. Where rules conflict, the priority order decides which wins. The console pushes it instantly over the persistent channel (or encrypted SMS if the device is offline), the on-device agent enforces it locally, and compliance shows up in Devices and Reports. Change the group, and every member follows.

Terms you will meet

Group, Zone and Bucket organize devices at different levels. Restriction, Policy and Order control behavior with priority. Kiosk, OTA, Watchdog, Geofence and App Limits are the headline features. Hover any dotted term anywhere in these docs for its one-line definition.
